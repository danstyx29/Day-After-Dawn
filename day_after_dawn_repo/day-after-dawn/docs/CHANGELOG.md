# Day After Dawn: Changelog

> **About this project:** Day After Dawn is an independent, non-commercial fan project inspired by *Project Zomboid*. All of its code, art, names and text are original. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by The Indie Stone, who own Project Zomboid. Mentions of Project Zomboid in this document are research and commentary.

*Every release from the first build to now. The newest is first, each with its date, what changed and how it was tested. Previously named Dead Quiet (renamed in v1.8). Last updated: 16 Oct 2026.*

### 1.13: performance (16 Oct 2026)

A patch only on frame rate, guided by the browser's CPU profiler across five scenarios: running through town, driving fast, zoomed out, a 60-zombie night chase in rain, and a late-game world with 121 explored areas.

**What was wrong:**
- **Advanced blend layers:** each frame stacked up to six full-screen soft-light, saturation and screen layers for colour grading, time of day and glare. A graphics card has to copy the whole screen before each one, and on a high-density screen that's millions of pixels several times a frame.
- **The night darkness layer:** drawn at full resolution.
- **Every zombie ever generated stayed in the live list:** in the late-game world that was 14,013 zombies, scanned several times a frame by the simulation, zombie updates and the hover label. The game got slower the more you explored.
- **The moving shadow layer (1.12):** redrawn every frame while walking.
- **Scans of the whole world:** the season calendar was recalculated for every tree, and lighting and building cleanup scanned every building and room.

**What changed:**
- **Grading in one layer:** the six blend layers are combined in code into one ordinary translucent layer plus the vignette. The look is kept: olive by day, amber at golden hour, blue at night.
- **Half-resolution night layer:** a quarter of the pixels, which doesn't show because lights are soft.
- **Automatic resolution:** if frames stay slow for 2.5 seconds, the internal resolution drops a step (1.5×, 1.25×, 1×, 0.85×, 0.7×), and it rises again after 12 smooth seconds.
- **Zombie storage, as in the original:** zombies more than 150 tiles away that aren't chasing you are stored with their area and restored when you come back. In the late-game world the live list fell from 14,013 to 960, with none lost and saves including the stored ones.
- **A sliding shadow layer:** drawn a quarter-screen larger than the view and slid as you move, redrawn only near its edge, on zoom changes or as the sun moves.
- **Lookups:** buildings are indexed by map area, rooms by building, and the calendar is cached per game day.

**Measured** in a headless browser that draws on the processor, so slower than a real machine with a graphics card. Median and slow (95th percentile) frames:

| Scenario | Before | After, fixed resolution | After, automatic resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Running through town | 113 / 157ms | 93 / 138ms | 55 / 70ms |
| Late-game world | 130 / 168ms | 116 / 182ms | 62 / 83ms |
| Driving fast | 110 / 178ms | 106 / 164ms | |
| Zoomed out | 123 / 166ms | 121 / 163ms | |
| 60-zombie chase | 146 / 202ms | 128 / 162ms | |

- **Frame rate:** with automatic resolution it roughly doubles, and no frame took over 100ms (before, 46 of 62 frames did when running through town).
- **Zombie code:** in the late-game world it dropped out of the profiler's top list.
- **Not measurable here:** the blend-layer savings land mostly on graphics cards, so expect the biggest gain on your machine.
- **Smoke test:** clean.

### 1.12: lighting, environment and ambience pass (16 Oct 2026)

- **The sun moves:** shadows swing from west at sunrise to east at sunset, stretch long when the sun is low, shorten at noon, soften under cloud and vanish at night. The old shadows were drawn at one fixed angle into the ground.
- **Colour by time of day:** dawn pink, golden hour, the blue hour, moonlit blue nights, greyer in rain.
- **Interiors lit by their windows,** as in the original's Build 42: windowless rooms are dark at noon, and a red sunset glow fills rooms with windows.
- **Eyes adjust:** step from light into darkness and you're half blind for a few seconds, clearing over about 20 seconds, with a brief glare going the other way.
- **Light colour:** cool fluorescent light spills from powered shops, cars throw headlight beams, and furniture sits on soft contact shadows. Cloud shadows drift across the ground on clear days.
- **Ground:** weeds grow in pavement cracks, and leaves lie under trees.
- **Life and motion:**
  - footprints in snow and mud, for you and nearby zombies
  - dust kicked up running on dry ground
  - falling leaves in autumn, fireflies on summer nights, bird flocks overhead
  - a glance around when you stand still
- **Ambience:**
  - **Continuous layers:** leaves in the wind, lapping water, wind between buildings (stronger high up), indoor room tone, and an underground drone.
  - **Occasional sounds:** woodpeckers, thrush trills, frogs, a ticking clock, dripping taps, and thumps from a basement.
  - **The first days:** distant screams and sirens, atmosphere only, so they attract no zombies.
- **Research:** three new rounds, R30 to R32 (the original's Build 42 lighting, ambience design, 2D lighting techniques).
- **Tested:**
  - **The sun:** checked hour by hour. At 7am shadows point west and are 3.2× long; at noon they're 1×; by 7pm they point east and are 2.6× long; at night there are none.
  - **Eye adjustment:** 0.45 extra darkness on stepping into the dark, clearing in about 20 seconds.
  - **Interiors:** of 114 house rooms, 45 are bright, 8 dim and 2 dark.
  - **Performance:** drawing time is within about 1ms of 1.11 in every scenario.
  - **Smoke test:** clean.

### 1.11: skylines and farms (16 Oct 2026)

- **High-rises:** bigger towns now get towers. Every town of eight or more blocks gets one, and about half get a second.
  - **Apartment towers:** 9 to 12 floors of flats.
  - **Office towers:** 9 to 14 floors of offices, storage and staff rooms.
  - **The hotel:** 8 to 10 floors of guest rooms over a lobby, restaurant and kitchen.
  - **Floors:** each is generated the first time you climb to it, furnished by type, with zombies on most floors and stairs up and down.
  - **Outside:** each tower has its own name and sign.
- **Tower stair cores:** tower ground floors are one open lobby, so the usual stair placement (which needs a wall beside it) found no spot. Without stairs, every tower fell back to a single storey. Towers now get a stair core: a short wall in the middle of the lobby with the staircase against it, as real towers have.
- **Farms:** about 70% of farms get a red barn (hay, crates and a workbench inside) and a white corrugated grain silo beside it.
- **Tested:**
  - **Towers:** 19 placed in the test area, all with stairs, all 9 to 14 floors.
  - **Climbing:** generating all 14 floors of the tallest office tower took 45ms in total. Its top floor had 4 furnished offices (31 objects) and stairs down.
  - **Farms:** 15 barns and 15 silos.
  - **Performance:** drawing next to the tallest tower took 7 to 11ms, in line with other town scenes.
  - **Smoke test:** clean.

### 1.10: tidy buildings, fill interiors, unstick, gentler engine (16 Oct 2026)

- **Stuck on the bed after respawning:** you could be placed on a tile that holds furniture, inside its collision.
  - **Spawning:** every new game and every "continue as a new survivor" now checks the spot and moves you to the nearest open floor.
  - **A watchdog:** if you ever end up inside something solid, it frees you within a second.
  - **Tested:** placed inside a bed, freed onto open floor.
- **Stray and misplaced walls:** the interior wall splitter sometimes ran a dividing wall right against a house's outer wall, leaving a double wall. The outer layer was orphan wall with no floor beside it. That's what rendered as odd brick slices sticking out and caused the strange collisions.
  - **The fix:** a tidy pass after each area generates removes the orphan layer, shrinks the building to its real size and gives the new outer wall windows.
  - **Result:** in the test area, 380 stray wall tiles across 52 buildings went down to none.
- **Empty buildings:** 7 of 12 hardware stores had no furniture at all (their room type had no furniture list). They now have shelving, counters and workbenches. The tidy pass also furnishes any room that came out empty: bedrooms, kitchens and bathrooms get their basics, and a tiny room gets one piece on the tile furthest from the door so it stays walkable. No building is empty in the test area now.
- **Fuller upper floors, without clutter:** rooms that are thin on furniture get thematic additions with walkways left clear.

  | Room type | What it gets |
  |---|---|
  | Offices | Rows of desks, plants, water coolers, filing cabinets |
  | Classrooms | Desk rows, bookcases |
  | Hospital wards | Rows of beds, medicine cabinets |
  | Fire station bunk rooms | Bunks, lockers |
  | Libraries | Rows of bookcases, reading tables |
  | Storage rooms | Shelving, crates |
  | Staff rooms | Tables, sofas, vending machines |
  | Mall concourses | Planters, benches |

  Rooms already well furnished are left alone. For example, office floors went from 1.5 to 2.1 objects per 10 tiles, wards from 2.0 to 2.6, and concourses from 0.8 to 1.3.
- **Car engine:** about 20% quieter, and softer (rounded waveforms through a lower filter, with smoother changes), so it hums rather than buzzes.
- **Research note:** the original's own map makers treat empty buildings and blank room definitions as bugs. Players also dislike furniture packed so tight you can't move, hence filled but walkable.

- **Footsteps carry like the original's.**

  | Movement | Noise reach |
  |---|---|
  | Walking | 4 tiles |
  | Running | 8 tiles |
  | Sneaking | 20% of walking |

  The Sneaking skill scales sneaking from 120% at level 0 down to 40% at level 10, so that's about 0.95 tiles at level 0 and 0.25 tiles at level 10. Lightfooted cuts every footstep from 99% down to 20% at level 10. Graceful (new trait) is 60%, Clumsy is 120%, and bare feet or snow make you quieter.
- **Zombies notice you gradually.** Each zombie builds awareness of you over time:
  - **Half aware:** it turns and shuffles over to look.
  - **Fully aware:** it chases.

  Awareness grows faster when you're more visible, central in its vision cone and close, and with the zombie's own perception, which varies from one zombie to the next. Feeding zombies barely notice anything.
- **Losing them works.** Break line of sight for a few seconds and a chasing zombie gives up following and searches where it last saw you. Chasing zombies moan, and nearby zombies follow the sound.
- **Visibility depends on:**
  - **Light:** daylight, street lamps, porch lights, candles, campfires, fires, and your own flashlight or torch.
  - **Stance:** crouching (×0.4, scaled by Sneaking skill), running (×1.3) or standing still (×0.85).
  - **Cover:** trees and bushes around you, and low cover between you and the zombie (fences, cars, hedges, crates, sandbags, counters) while sneaking.
  - **Weather:** rain and fog.
  - **Traits:** Inconspicuous ×0.5 and Conspicuous ×2, as in the original.
- **The eye icon** sits above the sound meter and reads Hidden, Concealed, Visible or Exposed. It turns orange at "Noticed..." and red at "Spotted!".
- **Light switches (L).** A lit room at night is bright inside and visible from outside through the windows. Switch the lights off to hide. Zombies hear the switch.
- **Silent takedowns.** With a knife, attack an unaware zombie at close range for a quiet kill. Behind it, sneaking and with Blades skill, the odds are better. If it fails, the zombie turns on you.

### 1.4: performance, interiors, architecture, systems (14 Oct 2026)


Saves from v1.3 won't load, because the world generator changed.

#### 1. Lag when entering new areas (top priority)

**What was wrong:** four things caused the hitches.
- **Region generation:** each new region was generated in one go, taking 20 to 55ms.
- **Wall images:** every wall tile had its own image, which meant thousands of separate images.
- **Image cache cap:** ground and roof images were capped at 160 blocks. A wide monitor zoomed out shows more than that, so the cache kept throwing images away and rebuilding them.
- **Tile scans:** the game scanned a square of tiles around you every frame, instead of only what's on screen.

**What's fixed:**
- **Region generation:** spread over many frames, about 4ms a frame, with its own random stream so the world still comes out the same every time.
- **Walls:** walls that look the same now share one image.
- **Ground:** baking happens within a time budget, with blocks ahead of you (in the direction you're moving) prepared in spare time. Anything not ready yet gets a cheap stand-in for a frame or two. Every frame still makes guaranteed progress.
- **Cache size:** it grows with your screen.
- **Tile visits:** only tiles actually on screen are visited.
- **Fog of war:** now one small, smoothly scaled image instead of thousands of rectangles.
- **Roofs:** they skip a full-screen layer when nothing needs cutting or fading.
- **Zoomed far out:** tiny details are left out.
- **Pathfinding:** no longer allocates new memory on every rebuild.

**Measured** walking into new land at full zoom-out in a software-rendered test browser:

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Worst frame | 125ms | 94ms |
| 95th-percentile frame | 95ms | 72ms |
| Frames over 100ms | 6 | 0 |

That's with far more detail on screen than before. A real graphics card does the heavy part (drawing pixels) much faster than this test.

#### 2. Upper floors now match the ground floor

**The cause:** the furniture placer refused any spot beside a window and required every window-side tile to stay reachable. Upper floors have windows every few tiles, so it placed almost nothing.

**The new interior designer** keeps doorways clear. Low furniture (beds, sofas, desks) can sit under windows, while tall furniture goes against blank walls. Every room gets:
- **Bedrooms:** bedside tables next to the bed, and rugs.
- **Living rooms:** armchairs, coffee tables in front of sofas, floor lamps, plants, and sometimes a fireplace or piano.
- **Kitchens:** dining tables with chairs.
- **Bathrooms:** tubs.
- **Offices:** desks with computers and water coolers.
- **Hospital wards:** IV stands.
- **Shops:** chairs at diner and bar tables.

**New furniture:** armchair, coffee table, nightstand, potted plant, bathtub, chair, dining table, office desk, rug, floor lamp (lights up when powered), fireplace, piano, water cooler, water heater, workbench, moving boxes, pantry shelf, IV stand, crib and dryer.

**Basements:** about 28% of houses have one. Take the stairs down to a dark concrete basement with shelves, a pantry, a workbench, a washer and dryer, a water heater and boxes. They're lit only when there's power.

#### 3. Neighbourhoods with soul

Research (ranch, farmhouse, Craftsman and colonial styles, and the original's towns) went into:
- **Pitched roofs:**
  - **Types:** real gable and hip roofs with overhanging eaves.
  - **Detail:** shaded slopes, shingle rows, ridge caps and gable-end walls trimmed in the house colour.
  - **Colours:** ten roof colours.
  - **Rooftop details:** chimneys sit on the roof slope.
  - **Other buildings:** churches have steep gables, motels hip roofs, and some diners, clinics, libraries, post offices, banks and fire stations get hip roofs too.
- **House styles:**
  - ranch (often brick below and siding above)
  - two-storey
  - cottage
  - bungalow with a big porch
  - white farmhouse
  - L-shaped houses with two-part roofs
- **House colours:** 14 siding colours, plus brick and brown brick, with five trim colours and shutters in six colours.
- **Porches:** deck boards, posts and a porch roof.
- **Garages:** detached, with roll-up doors, a driveway to the street, and often a car parked in it.
- **Paths and yards:** front walkways. Back yards get barbecues, lawn chairs, doghouses, clotheslines, bikes, garden gnomes, vegetable patches and garden sheds.
- **Lots:** varied widths, so a block reads as a street of different houses. Some blocks have back fences between rows.
- **Downtown:** shops get brick, stone, stucco or white fronts with dark or light trim, and many are two storeys with a flat above (like the bookstore flats in the original).
- **Gas stations:** a canopy over the pumps.

#### 4. Everything from the research survey

### Clothing, wetness and temperature
- **Garments:** 17 of them (shirts, sweaters, hoodies, jackets including leather, winter coats and raincoats, jeans, cargo and sweatpants, sneakers, boots, hats, gloves, a scarf). Each has insulation, wind and water resistance, and bite and scratch protection.
- **Wearing them:** wear or remove from the inventory with Use. You start in a t-shirt, jeans and sneakers.
- **Protection:** clothing on the body part that's hit can stop a bite or scratch, and it tears.
- **Wetness:** rain soaks you outdoors. Waterproof clothes help, wet clothes keep you less warm, and you dry off indoors or near a fire.
- **Body temperature:** it follows the air, clothing, wind, shelter and fire. You get chilly, then hypothermic (losing health), or overheated (thirst and tiredness).
- **Colds:** staying wet and cold can give you a cold for two days. Sneezing and coughing make noise that draws zombies.

### Seasons and weather
- **Seasons:** real seasons from the calendar, with temperatures modelled on Kentucky.
- **Day length:** winter days are shorter.
- **Snow:** when it's cold enough; it settles and slows walking.
- **Thunderstorms:** in warm months, with flashes. Thunder draws zombies from far away.
- **HUD:** shows the day, season and temperature.
- **Date:** the year now advances (it was stuck on 1993).

### Generators
- **Where:** found in garages, warehouses, auto shops and barns, or carried (very heavy).
- **Learning:** you need the "Generators for Dummies" magazine, Electrical 3 or the Mechanic job.
- **Power:** 20 tiles around it, including street lamps, porch lights, floor lamps and gas pumps.
- **Fuel and noise:** burns about 11% of a tank a day and is loud.
- **Danger:** running one indoors poisons you with carbon monoxide.

### Floors
- **Sheet ropes:** knot one from a bed sheet, then tie it to an upper window to climb down and back up. Zombies can't climb it.
- **Jumping:** you can jump from a window, but it risks a fracture. Make a splint (plank and rags) and apply it.
- **Stairs:** a sledgehammer can smash stairs to seal off the floor above.

### Environmental stories
- **Barricaded safehouses:** boarded up, better stocked and with more zombies inside. A hand-marked map in a nearby house shows where one is.
- **Other houses:** burnt-out houses (charred walls, black roof, gutted inside) and trashed houses that someone looted first.
- **On the roads:** highway crash sites, police blockades.
- **Wilderness:** camps with a tent, an old fire pit and a crate of supplies.

### Foraging
- **Search mode:** press V. Search radius grows with the new Foraging skill.
- **Finds:** branches, stones, berries, mushrooms, herbs and junk, with more in forests.
- **Conditions:** less in the dark and rain, and almost nothing to eat in winter.
- **Poison:** some berries and mushrooms are poisonous, less often as your skill grows. Finds are marked on the ground.

|---|---|---|---|---|

### Smaller realism details
- **Junk loot:** plates, forks, spoons, mugs, jars, razors, hair gel, rubber ducks and toy cars sit alongside the useful finds.
- **New items:** canned soup, candles (set down for eight hours of light), and torches (branch and rags, a short-lived light).
- **Lightfooted:** a new skill that quietens footsteps as you sneak near zombies. Going without shoes is quieter too.
- **Car alarms:** about 30% of cars have one, set off by smashing a window or sometimes by botched hotwiring.
- **Keys on zombies:** some car keys are in a nearby zombie's pocket and show up on its body.
- **Sunlight through windows:** gold in the morning, deep orange at sunset.
- **Quiet streets:** zombie spawns leave thin quiet "cracks" through towns, as in the original.
- **Corpse sickness:** standing near old corpses indoors makes you sick.
- **Muscle strain:** heavy fighting stiffens your muscles and weakens your swings until you rest.
- **Getting used to it:** you grow used to the dead over about five months, so panic builds more slowly.
- **The world grows over:** vines climb walls a little more each month.
- **Trees:** they shake as zombies push through them.

#### 5. Bugs fixed along the way
- **Roofs from upstairs:** the roofs of nearby houses disappeared when you were upstairs or when only pitched roofs were in view.
- **Roofs on slow frames:** roofs could fail to appear at all on a slow frame.
- **Loading mid-generation:** loading or starting a game while a region was half-generated corrupted the new world.
- **Missing saved fields:** saves now keep generators, ropes, map markers, the difficulty preset, day length, snow and fog.
- **Basement windows:** basements no longer get windows.
- **Fence tumbles:** zombies only tumble when crossing a fence, and always land on the far side.

#### What's still not in

Firearms (a big system), farming, fishing and trapping, fire spreading, a full vehicle mechanics screen, and the mall, cinema and gun store. These are good candidates for the next round.
